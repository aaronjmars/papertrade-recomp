### The two proxies are coupled: the set-2 Exchange needs the set-2 SessionKeyManager

The set-2 close path calls `SessionKeyManager.0xd95208cd` as a staticcall on every signed close (after a non-static `0xb002b9fa`), and the pinned SessionKeyManager does not dispatch that selector at all. A direct close then reverts with empty data; the same close inside a BatchExecutor batch fails silently, because the outer call still succeeds. Replaying the historical batch verbatim makes the difference visible:

| Run | Outer call | Exchange write slots | Historical positions still active |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| direct close, pinned SessionKeyManager | reverts (empty data) | 0 | n/a |
| direct close, set-2 SessionKeyManager | succeeds | 12 | n/a |
| historical batch, pinned SessionKeyManager | succeeds | 4 | 2 of 2 |
| historical batch, set-2 SessionKeyManager | succeeds | 22 | 0 of 2 |
| direct close, set-2 SessionKeyManager, slot-8 flag cleared | succeeds | 12 (identical set) | n/a |

So the wrapper masks the inner failure rather than the Exchange tolerating a failed verifier: no position is closed. Reproduce with `python3 scripts/audit_live2_authority.py --close-coupling` (five arms, each asserted, including the pinned-SessionKeyManager negative control); see `artifacts/live2_authority/close_coupling.json` and row `L2A-15`.

The hazard is not confined to closes: the signed-intents lane's differential shows the same silent-success shape on the deposit-sweep retry branch, where the set-2 implementation clears a dust pending word and returns success while the pinned one reverts `SweepBelowMinimum()` `0x329c2b0f` (cross-referenced in row `L2A-16`). That matters directly to the two live PT-01 pending sweeps.

### Who can attach a builder fee, and who can take one

* Through the registry, a fee cannot be turned on for an address by a third party: `approveBuilder` is owner-only and `feeRateOf` returns zero for anything unapproved.
* On the Exchange the close credits its slot-39 mapping at a site that keys on a word loaded from `mload(args_base + 0x180)`; the source of that word is not identified (row `L2A-09`), and the signed close struct carries no builder field (its `CLOSE_TYPEHASH` `0x9cc23a52...` is byte-identical to the pinned constant and hash-matches `Close(address user,uint256[] positionIds,uint256 nonce,uint64 deadline)`).
* `claimBuilderFees()` is callable by anyone, but it is strictly caller-scoped: it pays the caller's own entry and leaves every other entry intact, so a builder's accrual cannot be taken by another address (row `L2A-08`, disproved sub-claim).
* `0xd970fad5(a, b)` is the owner-only other half: it reassigns the accrual, moving `builderFees(a)` into `builderFees(b)` and leaving the shared slot-38 accumulator untouched, which shows slot 39 is an address-keyed balance (row `L2A-13`).
* Census of slot-39 touchers: only `claimBuilderFees` (its own caller key) and the owner reassignment. The sweep family does not reach that block — an earlier region-attribution lead of mine was disproved, and the row records it (row `L2A-14`).

signer B: multisig.confirmAndExecute(N, transactionHash(N))                 // 2 of 3 required

## Monitoring gap (C5)

An implementation swap writes the EIP-1967 slot with no event: the ProxyAdmin emits no upgrade event, the 925-byte shared proxy runtime contains no `LOG` opcode, and all eight proxy addresses returned zero `Upgraded` (`0xbc7cd75a...`) logs over blocks 49,063,457..49,255,838. An observer cannot detect the swap from logs. The cheap check is one `eth_getStorageAt(proxy, 0x360894a1...)` per proxy per poll, compared against the expected implementation, plus a code-hash comparison to catch a same-address change. Both checks are already implemented as `proxy_slots` in the capture file.

### `L2A-08` claimBuilderFees is permissionless but strictly caller-scoped

- Angle: C2 BuilderRegistry
- Precondition: The set-2 Exchange maps builder fees at slot 39; the slot-39 idiom is absent from the pinned implementation (0 occurrences of PUSH1 0x27), so the mechanism is new.
- Trigger: 0x69f9a5f0 claimBuilderFees() zeroes keccak256(abi.encode(msg.sender, 39)) and debits the shared slot-38 high 96 bits by that amount.
- Impact: The claim is not access-controlled, but it can only touch the caller's own entry, so no caller can take another builder's accrual: with entries 5e18 for A and 7e18 for B, A's call pays exactly 5e18 and leaves B's entry intact, B's pays exactly 7e18, the shared slot-38 counter falls by exactly the two amounts, and an address with no entry succeeds and is paid zero. Cross-caller theft is therefore disproved; the fee is not a bearer asset. An amount larger than the slot-38 counter reverts with a checked-arithmetic panic. The only entry points that touch slot 39 in any driven shape are this one (caller key) and the owner reassignment 0xd970fad5; the sweep family, the close and the open leave it alone.
- Evidence: testExchangeClaimBuilderFeesIsPermissionlessSelfClaim (outsider pays out its own entry, entry zeroed, slot-38 high 96 bits -= the amount, event 0x3416b71e..., overdraw panics 0x4e487b71, zero entry is a no-op success); peer exact-runtime rows L2-D2-04 in atlas/block_48902346/live2_intents.json (two seeded entries: each caller is paid only its own, the other survives); peer exact-runtime rows L2-D1-06 and L2-D2-04 in atlas/block_48902346/live2_intents.json (sweep family, close and open all leave the seeded slot-39 entries untouched); static: pinned Exchange PUSH1 0x27 count = 0; set2 count = 4

## Limits

- No archive HyperEVM endpoint is available: every live read is `latest` state at the head named in the capture file, not pinned state.
- The Forge replay is a port-free exact-runtime simulation with locally written storage; it sends no transaction and its HyperCore reads are never reached on a success path.
- Public-registry names are hash-matched candidates, never recovered Solidity source names.
- Three added selectors remain partly undecoded as to effect: Exchange `0xd970fad5` (guard and storage are proved, the economic meaning is not), library `0x06c5308a` and library `0xb9370332`.
