Known vectors 22

The Papertrade team documented 22 vectors as known. They are part of the protocol's intended design, managed through configuration, operations or governance rather than code changes, and are not counted among the findings.

Every known vector and how it is managed
IDVectorHow it is managed
Managed through protocol configuration
KV-01BBO midpoint manipulation

Opens, closes and liquidations execute at the instantaneous Hyperliquid BBO midpoint. PriceOracle rejects only a zero or crossed book, with no deviation, TWAP or lookback check, so a briefly distorted quote can be traded against.

The 0.2 bps win deadband, the asymmetric impact haircut on winning closes and per-side OI caps set by the market keeper, plus market retirement by the pause guardian. These reduce but do not remove manipulation profit.

KV-02Isolated position straddles

Opposing isolated positions, on one account or several, cap each losing leg at its margin while the winning leg keeps leveraged upside, so the LP is effectively short cheap optionality.

Max leverage, the per-position notional cap, the bust buffer and directional OI caps bound new exposure. There is no immutable aggregate exposure or loss bound.

KV-03Hard-bust liquidation

Crossing the bust trigger forfeits the full isolated margin even where a voluntary close would still return equity. At 1000x leverage a voluntary close at the trigger could return close to half the margin.

Max leverage, the bust buffer and the liquidation fee rate are configurable, and the trading interface discloses the hard-bust rule.

KV-04PAPER farming on gross losses

PAPER mints on each position's realised loss, on the full liquidated margin and on destroyed queue claims, not on net account loss, so hedged or debt-funded positions can mint PAPER at little net cost.

Mint curve parameters (flat rate, flat threshold and tail decay) bound emissions, and PAPER is non-transferable at launch.

KV-05LP-side fee extraction

Loss and liquidation fees are carved from the LP's gain, so offsetting positions can turn LP value into staker and dev fees with little net exposure. Loss fees also accrue without reserving for open winning positions.

Fee rates are owner-configurable, and position, leverage and OI limits bound the volume that can be cycled.

KV-06Staker LP-excess distribution

LP value above the staker reward cap is distributed without reserving unrealised winning PnL, and a dominant staker can recycle losses into USDC rewards.

stakerLpRewardCapUsd is set well above the flat PAPER region (documented at $5M).

KV-07Per-position size limit

maxUserPosition limits each position, not a user's aggregate exposure, so exposure can be split across many positions.

Directional OI caps and the per-position notional cap (documented at $10M) bound total exposure.

KV-08Dust queue entries

Very small queued claims keep the protocol in queue mode, which waives fees and blocks direct LP payouts until they are paid.

Minimum margin and minimum notional for opens, plus keeper harvest and queue compaction.
Managed through operations and manual intervention
KV-09Inferred deposit settlement

A deposit sweep is credited when the user's proxy balance decreases, not on proof that the Exchange received it. A failed or masked transfer is dropped and requested again.

The sweeper service monitors pending sweeps, the app sends one deposit at a time, and the owner credits missed deposits manually through reconcileSweepCredit.

KV-10Failed HyperCore withdrawals

withdrawToCore debits the internal ledger before the asynchronous CoreWriter transfer settles, and there is no on-chain retry.

An evidence-gated governance repair through reconcileSweepCredit, recorded in an append-only repair ledger, following the team's runbook.

KV-11Liquidation liveness

Liquidation is permissionless but pays no caller bounty, so third parties have no incentive to liquidate.

A dedicated, funded protocol keeper submits liquidations with priority fees, with heartbeat monitoring and alerting.

KV-12Staking reward timing

Staking rewards are cash-basis. Rewards queued during insolvency, staker fees pushed while the queue is live, and undistributed rewards go to whoever is staked when cash is distributed, so stake changes in between shift rewards.

Keepers push fees, distribute rewards and harvest the queue on a short cadence.

KV-13Queue tombstones

Debt-funded churn and compaction leave dead nodes in the global queue that harvest has to skip.

Keeper compaction and harvest cadence with a bounded maxEntries.

KV-14Unbounded intent prices

Signed open and close intents carry no price or slippage limit, so a submitter could hold one until an adverse price within its validity window.

Only the protocol's relayer keys can submit intents, each intent is valid for one hour, and session keys expire after 30 days.

KV-15Session key replay on replacement

A freshly deployed SessionKeyManager would accept old, unexpired registration signatures.

The manager is only upgraded in place. A replacement is treated as a new governed epoch that requires users to register again.

KV-16New market listing window

A newly added instrument starts with unlimited OI caps until the caps are set.

Markets are listed in a governed atomic batch behind the global opens gate, setting both caps before opens resume.

KV-17Terminal market retirement

Pausing a market is permanent, and remaining positions settle at the BBO snapshot captured at retirement. The global opens gate is the reversible alternative.

Pause guardian runbook. A retired market is listed again under a fresh instrument ID once its old OI is zero.

KV-18Governance and upgrade trust

The contracts are upgradeable proxies. The owner can upgrade implementations, change PAPER's auto-approved spender, swap relayers and keepers, and credit deposits manually.

Owner actions run through a timelock (documented at seven days) controlled by a multisig.

KV-19Off-chain dependencies

Trading, staking and deposits depend on the protocol's relayer, sweeper and keepers, and HyperCore precompile behaviour is mocked in the test suite.

Operated and monitored by the Papertrade team. These components were outside the scope of this review.
Accepted protocol semantics
KV-20Debt-funded margin is contingent

A queue claim used as margin stops being a senior queued liability while the position is open. It is re-enqueued at the back of the queue on close and destroyed on liquidation.

Documented settlement rule, covered by the team's tests.

KV-21Fee waivers under stress

Win fees are collected only when the queue is empty and the LP can cover the profit plus the fee. Loss and liquidation fees are waived while the queue is live.

Intentional user-favourable policy. It under-collects fees but does not overdraw the LP.

KV-22PAPER basis net of the LP-side fee

On a solvent losing close, PAPER mints on the realised loss less the LP-side loss fee.

Documented emission policy.

  1. H-01 High ExchangeSessionFee lets queue drain fees Logical Error Resolved
    Location
    queueHarvestAvailable()

    Description

    In ExchangeSessionFee, the profile adds a new reserved liability sessionFeeAccumulator, and correctly subtracts it in lpBankrollAvailable() But it does not override queueHarvestAvailable(), so harvest() inherits the base Exchange calculation that ignores sessionFeeAccumulator That makes unclaimed session-key fees look like queue backing cash Base `Exchange.queueHarvestAvailable()` reserves only

    totalBalances + totalLockedMargin + stakerFeeAccumulator + devFeeAccumulator

    It omits the session-fee reserve. meanwhile, lpBankrollAvailable() correctly adds + sessionFeeAccumulator, proving the session fee profile intended those funds to be reserved. harvest() then uses `queueHarvestAvailable()` and sends that available amount into `queue.harvest()`, which credits queue users internal balances and increases totalBalances Assume this state in ExchangeSessionFee

     treasury                 = 100

 sessionFeeAccumulator    = 100

 sessionKeyFees[feeAddr]  = 100

 totalBalances            = 0

 totalLockedMargin        = 0

 stakerFeeAccumulator     = 0

 devFeeAccumulator        = 0

 queue.totalQueued        = 100   // attacker is queue head

 sideBucket               = 0

    Correct behavior: queueHarvestAvailable() should return 0, because the only 100 in treasury is reserved for sessionKeyFees Actual behavior: inherited queueHarvestAvailable() returns 100, because it ignores sessionFeeAccumulator The attacker or anyone calls harvest(1) The queue pays the attacker 100 into balances[attacker], but sessionFeeAccumulator and sessionKeyFees[feeAddr] remain unchanged The same 100 is now double-counted, once as the attacker’s withdrawable balance, and once as an unclaimed session fee liability The attacker can then withdraw the harvested balance via `withdrawToCore`, which debits their internal balance and treasury

    Recommendation

    Override queueHarvestAvailable() in ExchangeSessionFee

    Resolution

    Papertrade Team: We confirm H-01 and accept High. The session-fee profile now includes sessionFeeAccumulator in the reserve calculation used by both LP availability and inherited queue harvest. The base calculation delegates to a virtual reserve function, and ExchangeSessionFee supplies the complete liability set. This prevents harvest from consuming cash backing an outstanding session-fee liability. The selected relayer-only profile remains unaffected.

  2. H-02 High Enqueued Wins Never Debit trackedLpUsd Logical Error Resolved
    Location
    https://github.com/GuardianOrg/exchange-contract-audit-guardian-team1-1780258247445/blob/48e552b2503236f6e2c17c2a2bed1c54504da409/contracts/src/Exchange.sol#L1364, https://github.com/GuardianOrg/exchange-contract-audit-guardian-team1-1780258247445/blob/48e552b2503236f6e2c17c2a2bed1c54504da409/contracts/src/PaperTokenomics.sol#L258-L259

    Description

    When a USD-funded winning close is insolvent, QueueLib.settleUsd() can split the winner’s PnL into directProfit and enqueuedAmount. Both values are LP liabilities: directProfit is paid immediately, while enqueuedAmount is owed through the queue. However, _applyBatchCloseAccumulator() only passes acc.directProfitCredit into PaperTokenomics.onCloseBatch(), omitting the queued winning amount. Note that this also contradicts the comments that (correctly) states the LP debit should be directProfit + enqueuedAmt. As a result, enqueued winning PnL never debits trackedLpUsd, and the LP accounting overstates real LP equity by exactly the omitted queued win. This phantom LP surplus later flows through stakerLpExcessToDistribute() / pushStakerFees() and distributes as real USD to stakers, draining funds that actually back users and LP liabilities. An attacker can trigger this by creating an insolvent winning close that enqueues profit, then temporarily draining the queue by opening a debt-funded position against their own queued claim. Once later losses settle as solvent, trackedLpUsd is credited without ever having been debited for the earlier queued win. The resulting phantom LP surplus flows into stakerLpExcessToDistribute() and is paid out through pushStakerFees(). In the PoC attached, trackedLpUsd - realLpEquity equals the omitted enqueued win exactly. The final pushStakerFees() call distributes more USD to the staker than the LP’s real equity, draining backing from user/LP funds rather than true LP profit.

    Recommendation

    Track queued winning PnL as an LP liability at enqueue time. Accumulate enqueuedAmount from insolvent winning closes alongside directProfitCredit, and pass directProfitCredit + enqueuedProfitCredit to PaperTokenomics.onCloseBatch() as LP-paid/owed profit. Also ensure queue harvest does not leave this debit unaccounted.

    Resolution

    Papertrade Team: We confirm H-02 and accept High. USD-funded wins now debit tracked LP equity by the complete economic liability: direct profit, queued profit, and applicable winner-fee liabilities. A memory-only accumulator keeps this tokenomics debit separate from user balance credit and from debt-funded reporting. Harvest does not debit the same liability again. This is a prospective accounting correction; production must use a fresh or provably pre-activation deployment unless a separately audited replay reconstructs cursor, PAPER supply, and tail progress together.

    Remediation Link to H-02

  4. M-02 Medium Liquidation sideBucket masks real LP gains Logical Error Resolved
    Location
    liquidatePosition()

    Description

    In liquidate, the insolvent-liquidation branch treats the entire liquidated margin as sideBucket whenever queue.totalQueued > 0, even if the queue only needs a tiny amount The excess is later released to LP cash, but PaperTokenomics is told lpCreditedUsd = 0 So dust queue makes large liquidations release untracked LP gains and bypass liquidation fees In _liquidatePosition, for USD funded positions, the code does

    if (queue.totalQueued == 0) {
    // charge liquidation fee
} else {
    sideBucket += size;
}

uint256 lpCredited = queue.totalQueued == 0 ? uint256(size) : 0;
paperTokenomics.onLoss(user, positionId, true, uint256(size), lpCredited);

    So a non empty queue, even a dust queue, forces the whole liquidated margin into sideBucket, skips the entire liquidation fee, and reports zero LP credit to tokenomics

    The problem is that harvest() later pays only the outstanding queue amount. If the queue clears and sideBucket still has a residual, the residual is simply zeroed, which releases it back into ordinary LP cash

    But PaperTokenomics.onLoss() only advances trackedLpUsd when lpCreditedUsd > 0; with lpCreditedUsd = 0, the real LP gain is not tracked

    Assume

    queue.totalQueued = 1 USDC
sideBucket = 0
liquidated margin = 1,000,000 USDC

    1. A liquidatable USD funded position with size = 1,000,000e18 is liquidated while queue.totalQueued > 0

    2. _liquidatePosition executes the insolvent branch

    sideBucket += 1,000,000e18

no liquidation fee is charged

paperTokenomics.onLoss(, mintBasisUsd = 1,000,000e18, lpCreditedUsd = 0)

    1. Anyone calls harvest()

    2. harvest() pays the 1 USDC queue claim, then sees the queue is cleared and zeroes the remaining sideBucket

    3. The remaining ~999,999 USDC becomes ordinary LP cash, but trackedLpUsd remains unchanged

    Correct behavior would only route the queue shortfall to sideBucket, the rest should be treated as LP credited liquidation gain and reported to tokenomics

    The Impact that this lets a tiny live queue distort the accounting of arbitrarily large liquidations

    Actual result that will happen LP receives almost all liquidated margin after harvest trackedLpUsd receives 0 liquidation fee receives 0 PAPER minting uses an artificially low lpStartUsd staker LP excess distribution is suppressed

    Because stakerLpDistributableExcessUsd() is derived from trackedLpUsd, any released but untracked LP gain will not become distributable to stakers. Also, since the mint curve uses lpStartUsd = trackedLpUsd, keeping trackedLpUsd artificially low can preserve favorable PAPER minting conditions across repeated liquidations

    Recommendation

    Before adding the liquidated margin to sideBucket, compute the actual uncovered queue shortfall, not just whether queue.totalQueued > 0

    Resolution

    Papertrade Team: We confirm M-02 and accept Medium. Every backed USD loss now credits tracked LP equity at settlement, net only of actual fee liabilities, regardless of temporary sideBucket routing. A queue-live liquidation credits the full forfeited margin; a solvent liquidation credits margin minus its fee. Harvest, debt consumption, and residual bucket release do not move the cursor again. The documented queue-live fee waiver remains unchanged. Because this is a prospective multi-ledger correction, production must use a fresh or provably pre-activation deployment unless a separately audited replay reconstructs tracked LP cursor, PAPER supply, and tail progress together.

    Remediation Link to M-02
  5. M-03 Medium Rounded debts create unclaimable staking rewards DoS Resolved
    Location
    PaperStaking._claimFor()

    Description

    PaperStaking can promise users slightly more rewards than it actually holds. even a 1 wei shortage is enough to make an honest user whole reward claim revert This happens because rewards are divided using integer math, which rounds down After users stake, unstake, and rewards are distributed, these tiny rounded values can add up incorrectly

    Reward per share is rounded down in _distribute() User reward debt is also rounded down in _stakeFor() and _unstakeFor() Claiming subtracts the full user amount from rewardReserve in _claimFor() creditStakingReward() requires enough staking balance in Exchange.sol

    The contract assumes

    total user rewards <= rewardReserve

    But because of rounding, this can become false by 1 wei Then _claimFor() does this

    rewardReserve -= amount;
exchange.creditStakingReward(user, amount);

    If amount is even 1 wei greater than rewardReserve, the subtraction reverts. the user receives nothing Example, an attacker uses tiny stake/reward amounts to create a 1 wei rounding deficit. After they claim first, the remaining rewardReserve is 1 wei too small for the victim claim, so the victim entire claim reverts. The attacker only gains dust, but the victim much larger reward claim can be blocked, An honest user may be unable to claim rewards because the contract is short by only 1 wei, the rewards are stuck until new rewards are added

    Recommendation

    make _claimFor reserve capped and leave unpaid dust pending so the user claims everything available and only the 1 wei shortfall remains pending

    Resolution

    Papertrade Team: We confirm M-03 and accept Medium. _claimFor now pays the lesser of the user's calculated reward and rewardReserve, retains only the unpaid remainder in pendingRewards, updates reward debt normally, and credits/emits/returns only the amount actually paid. The reserve cannot be overdrawn, and a one-wei rounding shortfall cannot block the backed portion of a claim. Any unfunded residual remains pending and can clear when later funding creates sufficient rounding slack. Staking weights, allocation, principal, and queue seniority are unchanged.

    Remediation Link to M-03
  6. M-04 Medium Tokenomics under debits gross winning PnL Logical Error Resolved
    Location
    applyBatchCloseAccumulator()

    Description

    Solvent winning closes under debit trackedLpUsd by the win fee, enabling fake LP excess distribution QueueLib.settleUsd() handles a solvent win as

    rawProfit = adjustedPnl
fee = rawProfit * winFeeRate
directProfit = rawProfit - fee
stakerFee + devFee = fee

    The code says the solvent win path pays rawProfit − fee to the user and accrues the fee to staker / dev accumulators But the actual LP bankroll delta is -rawProfit, not -(rawProfit - fee) The Exchange code own LP budget derivation confirms this, for a solvent win, the LP free delta is documented as −rawProfit, because both the user net profit and the fee reserve are liabilities against LP free cash However, _applyBatchCloseAccumulator() sends only acc.directProfitCredit to onCloseBatch() as lpPaidProfitUsd, directProfitCredit is post fee user profit, while feesFromLoss only corrects the loss branch; there is no equivalent feesFromWin or gross-win debit onCloseBatch() then debits trackedLpUsd only by lpPaidProfitUsd, and stakerLpDistributableExcessUsd() uses trackedLpUsd - stakerLpRewardCapUsd as the amount available for LP excess distribution

    Assume

    size = 1,000
adjustedPnl / rawProfit = 100
winFeeRate = 2%
fee = 2
directProfit = 98
queue.totalQueued = 0

    Actual Exchange accounting on close

    locked margin released:     +1,000 to LP-free calculation
user balance credited:      -1,098  (margin + directProfit)
fee accumulators credited:  -2
--------------------------------
actual LP-free delta:       -100

    But tokenomics receives

    lpPaidProfitUsd = directProfitCredit = 98
trackedLpUsd delta = -98

    So after this single close

    trackedLpUsd is overstated by 2 = the win fee

    With default fees, the drift is 2% of every solvent winning PnL. If governance sets winFeeRate to the allowed maximum, the drift can be up to 50% of every solvent winning PnL

    pushStakerFees() first claims real staker fees, then asks PaperTokenomics for LP excess, calls distributeLpToStakers(lpExcess), and pays or queues that amount to the staking recipient through _enqueueOrPay()

    Because trackedLpUsd is too high by cumulative solvent win fees, stakerLpDistributableExcessUsd() can report surplus that does not exist as LP profit. _enqueueOrPay() then turns that fake surplus into a real staker balance if LP cash is available, or a queue claim if it is not

    This is effectively a double allocation of winner side fees

    1. The win fee is already reserved in stakerFeeAccumulator / devFeeAccumulator
    2. The same fee amount is not debited from trackedLpUsd
    3. Later, inflated trackedLpUsd can cause the protocol to distribute that amount again as LP excess

    Recommendation

    we need to not reuse directProfitCredit as LP PnL, keep it only for user balance credit and add a separate accumulator for the tokenomics LP debit, for example

    uint256 lpPaidProfitForTokenomics;

    Then in the USD funded win branch

    if (isWin) {
    // Preserves the current design of excluding queued wins if desired.
    // For solvent wins, sfU + dfU is the win fee, so this debits rawProfit
    acc.lpPaidProfitForTokenomics += directProfit + sfU + dfU;

    // Existing event / user facing accounting can remain unchanged
    acc.totalProfitAfterFee += directProfit + enqueuedAmt;
}

    And In _applyBatchCloseAccumulator()

    paperTokenomics.onCloseBatch(
		user,
		acc.lpPaidProfitForTokenomics,
		mintBasis,
		lpCreditedFromLoss
);

    Resolution

    Papertrade Team: We confirm M-04 and accept Medium. USD-funded winners now debit tracked LP equity by direct profit, queued profit, and all actual winner-fee liabilities. User balance credit remains separate, and debt-funded closes retain their distinct incremental-profit semantics. Harvest does not debit the same liability again. The shared H-02/M-04 correction adds no storage and changes no user payout. Production must use a fresh or provably unaffected/pre-activation deployment unless a separately audited replay reconstructs tracked LP cursor, PAPER supply, and tail progress together.

    Remediation Link to M-04
  7. M-05 Medium Insolvent sideBucket losses Inflate paper Logical Error Resolved
    Location
    settleUsd()

    Description

    Related with M-02, L-10 The intended logic in the system, If user loses real money, the system gives them paper as compensation / reward This vulnerability could allow an external attacker to mint a huge amount of paper while losing almost no real USDC The vulnerable line is the insolvent loss branch in QueueLib.settleUsd()

    uint256 deduction = remainingLoss < size256 ? remainingLoss : size256;
sideBucketAdd = deduction;
marginOut = size256 - deduction;

    When queue.totalQueued > 0, a USD funded losing close does not credit the loss to LP and does not burn the user queued claim

    It parks the deducted margin in sideBucketAdd, returns the remaining margin, and later _applyBatchCloseAccumulator() mints paper on the full realized loss while setting lpCreditedFromLoss to zero for the sideBucketed slice

    That is already dangerous, but the critical path is that the parked loss can be atomically converted back into attacker available LP budget by consuming the tiny queue that triggered the insolvent branch, exploit

    1. Attacker has or creates a small queued claim Q, for example $1, so queue.totalQueued > 0
    2. Attacker closes a large USD funded losing position with loss L, because the queue is non empty, the snippet executes
    sideBucketAdd = L;
marginOut = size - L;

    The attacker loss is treated as an insolvent loss and mints paper, but it does not advance LP credit accounting

    1. Attacker opens a debt funded position of size Q _openPosition( useDebt=true ) consumes the attacker queue claim. If this drains queue.totalQueued to zero, exchange executes
    if (queue.totalQueued == 0 && sideBucket > 0) sideBucket = 0;

    The large sideBucket amount L is now released into ordinary LP bankroll

    1. Attacker closes the debt funded position and a winning USD funded position in the same close batch, ordering the debt funded close first The debt close re enqueues only the tiny claim Q, while the batch accumulator still has the released LP budget. The later USD-funded win sees queue.totalQueued > 0, so the win fee branch is bypassed, but it can still receive direct LP payment from the stale acc.lpBudgetRemaining
    2. Net result is that the attacker pays only about the tiny catalyst queue amount Q, preserves / recreates the queue claim, and receives paper minted on the large loss L

    Assume

    Q = 1 USDC queued claim
L = 1,000,000 USDC losing close
W = 999,999 USDC winning close

    close loser:
  sideBucketAdd = 1,000,000
  PAPER minted on 1,000,000 loss

debt open with Q:
  queue.totalQueued becomes 0
  sideBucket is zeroed/released into LP bankroll

batch close:
  debt close re-enqueues Q
  winning close direct-pays ~999,999 with no win fee

    The attacker USDC cost is approximately Q, while paper is minted as if they suffered a real L loss. This is effectively near free paper inflation, and because paper participates in staking / reward distribution, it can dilute honest stakers and route future usdc rewards to the attacker

    In other words, this vulnerability lets the attacker do

    1. Create a tiny unpaid queue claim, like $1.
    2. Close a huge losing trade, like $1,000,000, so the system mints PAPER as if they truly lost $1,000,000
    3. Use the tiny queue claim to unlock the sideBucket accounting
    4. Close another winning trade and get almost the same $1,000,000 back from the released LP budget.

    So the attacker net USDC cost can be close to the tiny queue amount, while they receive PAPER as if they took a huge real loss A note to include is that for the winning leg we do adjustedPnl = applyMarketImpact() In QueueLib, which slightly reduces the profit since the rollbit curve takes a haircut, but the attack is still profitable
